The Mandate

A Website Has More Than One Job.

I

Establish Confidence

The visitor must quickly understand who the company is, what it provides, and why it is credible.

II

Make the Offer Clear

Services, products, audiences, and next steps must be organized around the visitor’s decision — not the company’s internal language.

III

Support Discovery

Technical structure, content architecture, speed, indexing, schema, and internal linking should support search visibility.

IV

Convert Interest

Calls, forms, booking, checkout, downloads, and other actions must be clear, relevant, trackable, and easy to complete.

V

Support Operations

The platform may need integrations, dashboards, product management, notifications, automation, portals, or internal workflows.

VI

Remain Maintainable

The website must be secure, documented, editable, backed up, and capable of future expansion without constant reconstruction.

Diagnosis

The Website May Be Costing More Than It Appears.

A redesign is justified when the current platform prevents the business from communicating, acquiring, serving, or scaling effectively — not merely because the visual style is old.

The Core Conversion Web Method

One Continuous Path From Context to Improvement.

  1. Stage 1

    Business and User Context

    Objectives, users, offers, decision process, content, current platform, integrations, constraints, success criteria.

  2. Stage 2

    Information and Conversion Architecture

    Sitemap, page purpose, user journeys, content hierarchy, calls to action, conversion events, and required functionality.

  3. Stage 3

    Visual Direction and Prototype

    Design language, key-page composition, responsive behavior, components, accessibility, and approval checkpoints.

  4. Stage 4

    Development and Integration

    Frontend, CMS, backend, forms, data, analytics, integrations, performance, security, and content implementation.

  5. Stage 5

    Quality Assurance

    Responsive testing, browser testing, forms, links, accessibility checks, indexing controls, analytics validation, speed, and security review.

  6. Stage 6

    Launch and Stabilization

    Deployment, DNS or hosting coordination, backups, monitoring, handover, documentation, and post-launch correction period.

  7. Stage 7

    Improvement

    Optional ongoing SEO, conversion, maintenance, campaign, content, or development support.
